### q1

**Question:** Do SGLT2 inhibitors reduce the risk of hospitalization for heart failure in patients with heart failure and reduced ejection fraction?

## Effect of SGLT2 inhibitors on heart failure hospitalization in HFrEF

- In a randomized placebo-controlled trial of 4,744 patients with HFrEF (ejection fraction ≤40%), dapagliflozin reduced the risk of the composite of worsening heart failure or cardiovascular death [PMID31535829]. The abstract reports that SGLT2 inhibitors reduce the risk of hospitalization for heart failure in HFrEF [PMID34449189].
- However, the specific hazard ratio for heart failure hospitalization alone in HFrEF is not provided in the available evidence; the dapagliflozin trial abstract is truncated and does not report the hazard ratio for the primary composite or its components [PMID31535829].
- Absolute risk reduction and number needed to treat for heart failure hospitalization in HFrEF over 12–24 months cannot be calculated from the provided evidence because event rates and hazard ratios are not given [PMID31535829].
- Subgroup analyses by diabetes status, ejection fraction, or background therapy (e.g., sacubitril/valsartan, beta-blockers) are not described in the available evidence [PMID31535829].
- The effect on the composite of cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization and on all-cause mortality in HFrEF is not reported in the provided evidence; the dapagliflozin trial abstract is incomplete [PMID31535829].
- Safety data (e.g., genital mycotic infections, diabetic ketoacidosis, volume depletion, amputation) in HFrEF populations are not provided in the available evidence [PMID31535829].

## Context from other heart failure populations

- In HFpEF (ejection fraction >40%), empagliflozin reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.69–0.90) [PMID34449189], and dapagliflozin also reduced worsening heart failure or cardiovascular death [PMID36027570].
- A meta-analysis in HFmrEF/HFpEF pooled data from six RCTs and 15,769 patients, but the abstract is truncated and does not provide pooled hazard ratios for heart failure hospitalization [PMID36914068].

## Conclusion

The available evidence supports that SGLT2 inhibitors reduce heart failure hospitalization in HFrEF [PMID31535829, PMID34449189], but the magnitude, absolute benefit, subgroup consistency, mortality effect, and safety profile in HFrEF cannot be determined from the provided abstracts.

## Does weekly semaglutide reduce cardiovascular events in adults with obesity without diabetes?

### Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE)
- In the SELECT trial, weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced the primary MACE endpoint (cardiovascular mortality, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke) by 20% versus placebo in adults with overweight/obesity and established cardiovascular disease but without diabetes [PMID38907684].
- A prespecified analysis of SELECT confirmed a significant reduction in MACE incidence with semaglutide compared with placebo among 17,604 patients, with consistent benefits across all baseline weight and waist circumference categories [PMID41138739].

### Cardiovascular and all-cause mortality
- The evidence provided does not report specific effects of semaglutide on cardiovascular death or all-cause mortality in adults with obesity without diabetes; the SELECT primary endpoint included cardiovascular mortality as a component, but individual mortality outcomes are not detailed in the cited abstracts [PMID38907684].

### Heart failure and other cardiovascular outcomes
- A prespecified analysis of SELECT examined ischaemic and heart failure cardiovascular outcomes in patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and prevalent heart failure, but the abstract does not provide the results of this analysis [PMID39181597].
- The SELECT analysis of HbA1c also evaluated an expanded MACE (including coronary revascularization or hospitalization for unstable angina) and a heart failure composite, but the abstract does not report the effect sizes for these outcomes [PMID38907684].

### Variation by baseline risk factors
- Cardiovascular benefits of semaglutide were consistent across all baseline weight and waist circumference categories in SELECT [PMID41138739].
- The evidence does not provide data on how benefits vary by age, sex, or pre-existing cardiovascular disease status in this population.

### Weight loss and cardiometabolic risk factors
- The evidence does not report the magnitude of weight loss or changes in blood pressure, lipids, HbA1c, or inflammation with semaglutide in adults with obesity without diabetes, nor how these relate to cardiovascular event reduction [PMID38907684][PMID41138739].

### Summary
- Weekly semaglutide reduces MACE in adults with obesity and established cardiovascular disease but without diabetes, with consistent benefit across adiposity categories [PMID38907684][PMID41138739].
- Data on mortality, heart failure outcomes, and cardiometabolic risk factor changes in this specific population are insufficient in the provided evidence.

### q3

**Question:** How much does statin therapy reduce major vascular events per 1 mmol/L reduction in LDL cholesterol in primary prevention?

## Relative Risk Reduction in Primary Prevention
- In primary prevention populations, statin therapy reduces major vascular events by approximately 21% per 1 mmol/L reduction in LDL cholesterol, based on a meta-analysis of trials in people with diabetes (which included both primary and secondary prevention) [PMID20171948].
- For stroke specifically, statins produce a 17% proportional reduction in first-ever stroke per 1 mmol/L LDL cholesterol reduction, with absolute reductions of 5 fewer strokes per 1,000 in primary prevention participants over 5 years [PMID17596685].

## Primary vs Secondary Prevention
- The relative risk reduction per 1 mmol/L LDL cholesterol reduction appears similar in primary and secondary prevention settings, though the evidence is indirect: the 21% reduction in major vascular events was consistent across diabetes types and prevention contexts [PMID20171948], and the 17% stroke reduction was observed in both primary and secondary prevention populations [PMID17596685].
- Direct comparisons of the magnitude of effect between primary and secondary prevention are not provided in the available evidence.

## Absolute Risk Reduction by Baseline Risk
- Absolute risk reduction depends on baseline cardiovascular risk: in primary prevention, 5 fewer strokes per 1,000 over 5 years, compared with 8 fewer per 1,000 in those with preexisting coronary artery disease [PMID17596685].
- No data are available to stratify absolute risk reduction for major vascular events by finer baseline risk categories in primary prevention.

## Variation by Statin Type, Dose, or Baseline LDL
- The proportional reduction in major vascular events per 1 mmol/L LDL cholesterol reduction does not appear to vary by statin type or dose intensity, as the meta-analysis weighted effects per 1 mmol/L reduction across trials [PMID25579834].
- Baseline LDL cholesterol level does not modify the proportional reduction, based on consistent effects across trials [PMID25579834].

## Time Horizon of Benefit
- Benefits accrue over an average of 5 years of treatment, with stroke reductions observed over this period [PMID17596685].
- The meta-analysis of 27 trials (including 22 statin vs control and 5 more-intensive vs less-intensive trials) weighted effects per 1 mmol/L LDL reduction, but the abstract does not specify the time course of major vascular event reduction [PMID25579834].

## Summary
- In primary prevention, statin therapy reduces major vascular events by about 21% per 1 mmol/L LDL cholesterol reduction, with absolute benefits proportional to baseline risk (e.g., 5 fewer strokes per 1,000 over 5 years) [PMID20171948][PMID17596685].

### q4

**Question:** What is the effect of intensive blood pressure lowering to below 130/80 mmHg on cardiovascular events?

## Effect of Intensive Blood Pressure Lowering to Below 130/80 mmHg on Cardiovascular Events

- **Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE):** Intensive blood pressure lowering has been associated with significant reductions in major cardiovascular events in meta-analyses of randomized trials [PMID30948822]. However, the specific target of below 130/80 mmHg is not directly evaluated in the available evidence; the evidence primarily compares intensive versus standard targets, with some trials using systolic targets <120 mmHg [PMID30948822].

- **All-cause and cardiovascular mortality:** Intensive blood pressure lowering may reduce all-cause mortality, but the evidence is not consistent across all trials [PMID30948822]. The effect on cardiovascular mortality is less clear from the available abstracts.

- **Stroke and heart failure:** Intensive blood pressure lowering reduces the risk of stroke and heart failure, with effect sizes reported in meta-analyses [PMID30948822]. However, specific effect sizes for a target below 130/80 mmHg are not provided in the evidence.

- **Serious adverse events:** Intensive blood pressure lowering is associated with a modest increase in renal events, but the incidence of hypotension, syncope, acute kidney injury, and electrolyte abnormalities is not detailed in the available evidence [PMID30948822].

- **Subgroup differences:** The evidence for subgroups such as older adults, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and prior cardiovascular disease is limited. A Cochrane review protocol on blood pressure targets in chronic kidney disease indicates that the optimal target remains unknown and that lower targets may increase adverse events [PMID39403990]. No specific subgroup analyses for a target below 130/80 mmHg are available in the provided evidence.

**Limitations:** The evidence is insufficient to directly answer the question for the specific target of below 130/80 mmHg. Most data come from meta-analyses comparing intensive versus standard targets, often with systolic targets <120 mmHg or <130 mmHg, and the abstracts do not provide detailed effect sizes or adverse event rates for the 130/80 mmHg threshold. Further research is needed to clarify the benefits and harms of this specific target.

### q6

## Does low-dose colchicine reduce cardiovascular events in chronic coronary artery disease?

**Efficacy in chronic coronary disease**
- In the LoDoCo2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 5,522 patients with chronic coronary disease, colchicine 0.5 mg once daily reduced the primary composite end point of cardiovascular death, spontaneous myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, or ischemia-driven coronary revascularization over a median follow-up of 28.6 months [PMID32865380].
- The benefit appeared consistent regardless of prior ACS status or timing of prior ACS, based on interaction analyses in LoDoCo2 [PMID34446156].
- A Cochrane review notes that low-dose colchicine has been proposed for secondary prevention in high cardiovascular risk patients, but that a previous review found considerable uncertainty about benefits and harms [PMID41224205].

**Magnitude of effect**
- The available evidence does not provide the specific relative or absolute risk reduction figures for LoDoCo2 in the retrieved abstract text; these numbers cannot be stated from the supplied evidence [PMID32865380].

**Time horizon**
- The median follow-up in LoDoCo2 was 28.6 months [PMID32865380]; the evidence provided does not specify when the benefit first emerged or whether it persisted beyond this period.

**Safety and discontinuation**
- The provided evidence does not report rates of gastrointestinal intolerance, infection, or discontinuation for low-dose colchicine versus placebo in chronic coronary disease [PMID32865380].

**Subgroups and ACS comparison**
- LoDoCo2 analyses found colchicine's benefit consistent independent of prior ACS status [PMID34446156].
- In ACS populations, the role of colchicine remains uncertain due to inconsistent trial outcomes, despite ESC endorsement for chronic coronary syndrome [PMID41023831].

**Bottom line**
- Low-dose colchicine (0.5 mg daily) reduced MACE in chronic coronary disease in LoDoCo2 [PMID32865380], with consistent effects across prior ACS timing [PMID34446156]; however, effect-size details, safety data, and long-term persistence are not established by the provided evidence [PMID32865380][PMID41224205].

## Weight loss with once-weekly semaglutide in STEP trials

- In STEP 1, among adults with overweight/obesity without diabetes, once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg plus lifestyle intervention produced a mean weight loss of 17.3% (SD 9.3%) from week 0 to week 68, compared with 2.0% (SD 6.1%) with placebo [PMID35441470]. The trial enrolled 1961 adults with BMI ≥30 (or ≥27 with a weight-related condition) and used coprimary endpoints of percentage weight change and ≥5% weight reduction [PMID33567185].
- After treatment withdrawal in the STEP 1 extension, participants regained 11.6 (SD 7.7) percentage points of lost weight by week 120, resulting in a net loss of 5.6% (SD 8.9%) from week 0 to week 120, while placebo participants had a net loss of 0.1% (SD 5.8%) [PMID35441470].
- In STEP 4, a withdrawal design, 902 participants received once-weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg during a 20-week run-in; 803 (89.0%) continued and were randomized to continued semaglutide or placebo for weight maintenance through 68 weeks [PMID33755728]. The abstract does not report the magnitude of weight loss achieved during the run-in or the between-group difference in weight maintenance.
- The provided evidence does not include results from STEP 2 (adults with type 2 diabetes) or STEP 3 (intensive behavioral therapy), nor does it report the proportions of participants achieving ≥5%, ≥10%, ≥15%, or ≥20% weight loss across STEP trials, or how weight-loss magnitude varied by baseline BMI, sex, or dose-escalation schedule. These questions cannot be answered from the supplied evidence.
- A separate phase 3b trial (STEP UP) evaluated semaglutide 7.2 mg versus 2.4 mg and placebo in adults with obesity; the abstract states that 7.2 mg was superior to placebo and 2.4 mg for bodyweight, but it does not provide the 2.4 mg weight-loss results [PMID40961952].

### q8

**Question:** Does dapagliflozin reduce kidney disease progression in chronic kidney disease?

## Does dapagliflozin reduce kidney disease progression in CKD?

- **Composite kidney outcome:** In the DAPA-CKD trial, dapagliflozin significantly reduced the risk of the primary composite outcome (≥50% eGFR decline, end-stage kidney disease, or kidney-related death) compared with placebo (9.2% vs 14.5%; HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.51–0.72; P<0.001) [PMID32970396].
- **Effect by diabetes status and CKD cause:** A prespecified analysis confirmed that dapagliflozin reduces the risk of kidney failure and heart failure in patients with CKD, both with and without type 2 diabetes, and across different underlying causes of CKD [PMID33338413].
- **Specific subgroup (IgA nephropathy):** In participants with IgA nephropathy, dapagliflozin reduced the risk of kidney failure and prolonged survival [PMID33878338].
- **End-stage kidney disease/kidney replacement therapy:** The composite outcome included end-stage kidney disease, and the significant reduction implies a lower progression to ESKD [PMID32970396].
- **eGFR slope and time course:** The available evidence does not provide detailed data on the rate of sustained eGFR decline (slope) or the time course of kidney benefit; these are not reported in the cited abstracts [PMID32970396][PMID33338413].
- **Variation by baseline eGFR/albuminuria:** The trial enrolled patients with eGFR 25–75 mL/min/1.73m² and albuminuria 200–5000 mg/g, but the abstracts do not report effect modification by baseline eGFR or albuminuria levels [PMID32970396][PMID33338413].
- **Risks (AKI, hyperkalemia):** The cited abstracts do not provide data on acute kidney injury or hyperkalemia risks with dapagliflozin in CKD trials [PMID32970396][PMID33338413].

**Conclusion:** Dapagliflozin reduces the risk of a composite kidney outcome in adults with CKD, with consistent benefits regardless of diabetes status and across CKD causes, including IgA nephropathy. However, evidence on eGFR slope, effect modification by baseline eGFR/albuminuria, and specific safety outcomes (AKI, hyperkalemia) is insufficient in the provided abstracts.

### q9

**Question:** Do SGLT2 inhibitors benefit heart failure with preserved ejection fraction?

## Do SGLT2 inhibitors benefit heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)?

**Randomized evidence supports a benefit of SGLT2 inhibitors in HFpEF, primarily by reducing heart failure hospitalizations.**

- In EMPEROR-Preserved (n=5988, EF >40%), empagliflozin reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization over a median of 26.2 months (13.8% vs 17.1%; HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.69–0.90; P<0.001) [PMID34449189]. This effect was mainly driven by a lower risk of hospitalization for heart failure [PMID34449189].
- In DELIVER (n=6263, EF >40%), dapagliflozin also targeted the composite of worsening heart failure or cardiovascular death over a median of 2.3 years; the primary outcome occurred in 16.4% of dapagliflozin patients [PMID36027570]. The abstract does not provide the full effect estimate or between-group comparison, so the magnitude of benefit from DELIVER cannot be fully cited here [PMID36027570].
- A prespecified meta-analysis of DELIVER and EMPEROR-Preserved, together with trials in reduced EF and worsening HF, was performed to examine cardiovascular mortality and subgroup effects [PMID36041474]. However, the abstract does not report the pooled effect sizes for the HFpEF population, so the meta-analytic estimate cannot be stated from the available evidence [PMID36041474].
- A review notes that in EMPEROR-Preserved the benefit was driven by a reduction in worsening HF events and concludes that SGLT2 inhibitors are likely to become the new standard of care in HFpEF [PMID36216487].

**Gaps in the evidence provided:**
- The effect on cardiovascular and all-cause mortality in HFpEF is not quantified in the available abstracts [PMID34449189][PMID36027570][PMID36041474].
- Effects on quality of life, functional status (e.g., KCCQ, 6-minute walk), or symptoms are not reported in the provided evidence [PMID34449189][PMID36027570][PMID36041474][PMID36216487].
- Consistency of benefit across subgroups (ejection fraction range, diabetes status, kidney function) and safety/adverse events are not detailed in the available abstracts [PMID34449189][PMID36027570][PMID36041474][PMID36216487].

**Conclusion:** SGLT2 inhibitors reduce the composite risk of cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization in HFpEF, with a hazard ratio of 0.79 for empagliflozin, driven mainly by fewer heart failure hospitalizations [PMID34449189][PMID36216487]. Dapagliflozin also showed a primary outcome rate of 16.4% in DELIVER, but the comparative effect size is not available in the abstract [PMID36027570]. Further data are needed to quantify mortality, quality-of-life, and subgroup effects [PMID36041474].

## Does adding ezetimibe to statin therapy further reduce cardiovascular events?

- **Randomized evidence:** In IMPROVE-IT, adding ezetimibe to simvastatin in patients after acute coronary syndrome reduced the primary composite of cardiovascular death, major coronary events, and stroke compared with simvastatin monotherapy [PMID26039521]. The benefit was enhanced in patients with diabetes mellitus [PMID29263150].
- **Magnitude of relative risk reduction:** The abstract of IMPROVE-IT does not report the exact relative risk reduction; it states that the combination reduced the primary end point [PMID26039521]. The diabetes subgroup analysis indicates enhanced benefit but does not provide a numeric relative risk reduction in the abstract [PMID29263150].
- **LDL-C lowering and attributable benefit:** The evidence does not specify the additional LDL-C lowering achieved by ezetimibe or quantify how much of the cardiovascular benefit is attributable to incremental LDL reduction [PMID26039521][PMID29263150].
- **Patient populations:** The greatest benefit was observed in patients with diabetes mellitus after acute coronary syndrome [PMID29263150]. An exploratory analysis of IMPROVE-IT assessed patients with polyvascular disease and type 2 diabetes, but the abstract does not report the effect of ezetimibe in these subgroups [PMID30396865].
- **Time course, absolute risk reduction, and NNT:** The evidence does not provide the time course over which event reduction becomes apparent, absolute risk reductions, or numbers needed to treat [PMID26039521][PMID29263150].
- **Safety:** The evidence does not report adverse events, discontinuation rates, or hepatic/muscle effects of ezetimibe added to statin therapy relative to statin monotherapy [PMID26039521][PMID29263150].

**Limitations:** The available abstracts are insufficient to answer several sub-questions (magnitude of relative risk reduction, LDL-C attribution, time course, absolute risk reduction/NNT, and safety). A full-text review or additional trials would be needed.

## Is Low-Dose Aspirin Effective for Secondary Prevention After a Cardiovascular Event?

### MACE Reduction
- Low-dose aspirin is of definite and substantial net benefit for many people who already have occlusive vascular disease [PMID19482214].
- In secondary prevention trials, aspirin allocation yielded a proportional reduction in serious vascular events (myocardial infarction, stroke, or vascular death) [PMID19482214].

### Bleeding Risk
- The evidence indicates that low-dose aspirin is associated with major bleeds, but the abstract does not provide specific risk estimates for secondary prevention populations [PMID19482214].

### Net Clinical Benefit Across Populations
- The net benefit of low-dose aspirin is established for patients with occlusive vascular disease, but the comparative net benefit across post-MI, post-stroke, and peripheral artery disease populations is not detailed in the provided evidence [PMID19482214].

### Optimal Regimen
- The evidence does not specify the optimal low-dose aspirin regimen (75-100 mg vs higher doses) or duration for secondary prevention after a cardiovascular event [PMID19482214].

### Comparison with Other Antiplatelet Strategies
- Clopidogrel monotherapy has been compared with aspirin monotherapy in patients with established coronary artery disease, but the abstract does not report the results of this comparison [PMID40902613].
- P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy has been compared with aspirin, with one study suggesting superior efficacy and similar overall safety for P2Y12 inhibitors, but the abstract does not provide specific data [PMID37407118].
- Another meta-analysis assessed the long-term comparative effectiveness of P2Y12 inhibitors versus aspirin, but the abstract does not report the findings [PMID40467090].

### Summary
- Low-dose aspirin is effective for secondary prevention of vascular events in patients with occlusive vascular disease [PMID19482214].
- The evidence is insufficient to determine the optimal dose, duration, or comparative net benefit across different secondary prevention populations, or to fully compare aspirin with other antiplatelet strategies [PMID19482214][PMID40902613][PMID37407118][PMID40467090].
